# Audit, 5 November 2026: every flow, by role

Colly asked on 5 November for a functionality, UI and UX audit of the
whole app and of each of its user flows. This is that audit. It looks at
how the app works and feels for each kind of person (director, office,
head of department, warehouse, staff crew, freelancer, and the system
admin), on a phone and at a desk, in dark and light. It is not a code or
security audit. Code is cited only as evidence of where a screen decides
something.

It audits main at **19ed275, app 1.77.0**, after the purchase list on a
freelancer's link ([ADR 0108](adr/0108-the-purchase-list-on-a-freelancers-link.md)).
Since the [20 October audit](audit-2026-10-20.md), whose 115 findings were
all fixed by 30 October apart from 89 (invoice details), the app has gained
picking on the phone, job tasks and stages with one Next button, racks and
the rack builder, the purchase list, the whole quoting engine (rate rules,
packages, travel, margin, the printed quote, sending by email), Claude
inside the app, the Quotes page, the system status page, certificates on
items, cable labels, filter and sort on long lists, trackers, and the
Security tab's limits ([ADRs 0072 to 0108](adr/)). Most of what follows is
about that newer work. Nothing here repeats a finding that is already
fixed. Where an earlier one is still open, it says so.

Nothing has been fixed. Colly chooses what to fix, and the
[order to fix things](#the-order-to-fix-things) at the end is a
suggestion.

**How it was done.** There were ten reviews, one for each group of flows.
Each walked its flows end to end as each role, on a local copy of the app
filled with made-up data at about a third of live size: 300 jobs, 542
phases, 389 crew calls, 767 offers, 60 people, 200 products, 1,500 items, 80
quotes and 4 vans, plus the hand-made set the browser tests use. They used
a phone (390×844) and a desk (1440×900), in both themes, read every word,
counted taps and screenshotted what they reported. Where a list's length
mattered, they also ran a copy at full size (900 jobs, 100 people, 3,000
items). Each review's findings then went to a separate sceptic. The
sceptic opened the same screen on a copy of its own, as the same role,
read the code and the decision records, and confirmed, refuted or
re-weighed each one. Only findings that survived are here.

Nothing live was read or changed. Nothing went near Google, Gmail,
Railway or Neon, nothing was written to a calendar, and nobody was
contacted. Ask Claude, the operations mailbox and Google sign-in need
keys the local copy doesn't have, so for those the audit judged the
"off" and "not set up" states, which is what live shows until they are set
up. The system admin role needs Google sign-in, so it was judged from the
code. The four-core audit machine was shared by up to ten reviews at once,
so every timing given is an upper bound.

Screenshots are in the project folder under `screens/audit-ux/`, one folder
per review plus one per sceptic (`verify-…`). They show made-up data. The
"Seeing it as … made-up data" line under the company name in them is how
the local copy plays each role. The live app shows only the company name.

## The short version

**103 findings: no P0, 12 P1, 43 P2, 48 P3.** The ten reviews walked 142
flows and reported 112 findings. Nine were found by two reviews and are
written once, and four were refuted because the decision records chose
them on purpose. The sceptics confirmed 60 as written and re-weighed 48.

What works is a long list, and it is in [What held up](#what-held-up). In
short: every screen has one heading and one width, and nothing scrolls
sideways on a phone. A tap reaches the next screen in under a second even
with the processor slowed four times. Every count on a director's and a
head's Home opens the screen that explains it. The switches say plainly
that the app contacts nobody, and anything that can't be undone asks first.
The whole pick-and-scan walk works offline and goes up within a second of
the signal coming back. A typed scan answers in under 70 ms. Numbers on a
quote agree from the draft to the PDF, the Quotes page, Money and Home. The
freelancer's page weighs about 9 KB and opens in half a second on slow 3G.

What gets in the way falls into six patterns:

1. **The next thing happens out of sight.** The job page's Next button on a
   phone switches to a tab about 1,000 px below, so nothing seems to happen
   (finding 1). Confirming an answer on Crew › Calls opens its follow-up at
   the foot of a 16,000 px page (5). On the freelancer's link, the sticky
   bar covers the message the page jumps to (7), and the days-off thank-you
   is drawn 7,000 px above where the page lands (31). Opening a product
   from the catalogue loses your place (9). The app does the right thing,
   but people can't see it, so they press again or give up.
2. **Records that disagree about where a job is.** A past job nobody
   ticked as deprepped stays in Back for good (3). About 108 sit there in
   the local copy, so expect 300 or more on live. A show whose kit wasn't
   scanned says "Back in: not needed" beside "Picked 0 of 14" (4). Kit and
   vans settled never ticks itself (22). Undo on a scan is counted as kit
   coming back (8). Next still says "Record the client's answer" after the
   client said yes (25). Quotes for jobs that have happened still say
   "Waiting on the client" (18). The five stages from the 1 November
   research are in, but what feeds them still contradicts itself.
3. **Heads see the whole company.** Crew needed starts on a head's
   department, but Answers to check and the Crew badge don't: they show 76
   when Home says 8 (6). Nor do the leave queue (28), People (42), the offer
   picker (62) or the certificates count (47).
4. **Buttons and words for things you can't do.** Staff crew get the job
   page's one red button for booking crew and picking kit (2). Elsewhere
   they are told to start counts (74) and to add calendars they can't add
   (78). Email quotes can be switched on with no mailbox, and Claude with
   no key (49, 81).
5. **Changes made quietly that the person didn't see coming.** A crew-list
   re-run replaces someone's email and throws the old one away, so the next
   calendar bring-in makes them twice (10). A refused role change snaps back
   and is listed as "a change from an older version of the app" (11).
   Picking a role applies it at once (39). Switching a quote line from
   Daily to Weekly drops its price by two thirds (23). Combining two people
   drops a phone number without saying so (43).
6. **Long pages at real size.** Jobs › Board runs 38,000 to 59,000 px
   because Back draws every job (3). An 81-line pick list is 20 phone
   screens (35). Quotes, Money and the busiest freelancer's page are 10 to
   16 phone screens (53, 32). A full bay's rolling count draws all 592
   numbers (38), and Testing names 260 products in two paragraphs (36).

On a phone, two things hurt every day. The week ahead on Home hides the
name of exactly the jobs that are short of crew or kit (12). Faded counts
on tabs and the "Hand it to someone" link fail contrast in both themes (54,
55).

## The flows

The flows the reviews walked, grouped as the audit was, with how each goes
today in a line or two. The roles in brackets are the ones the flow was
walked as. The findings that follow name the flow they come from.

- **The job flow, enquiry to deprep:** new job, client and venue, phases,
  the five stages and their tasks, the Next button, confirming or losing
  the job, cancelling and reopening, the Jobs list, the Board, the
  planner, a job from a past Current RMS quote, Your tasks.
- **Quoting and money:** starting a quote, adding lines with the product
  picker, rate rules, discounts, an agreed total, packages, travel and
  subsistence, availability, subhire and margin, the printed quote,
  sending by email (off), the Quotes page, past Current RMS quotes, Ask
  Claude (off), the client's answer, invoiced and paid, Jobs › Money and
  prices.
- **Crew booking:** asking for crew, Crew › Calls, offering (messages off
  and on), answers to check, counters, the call sheet, running late, To
  sort out, leave and days in lieu, timesheets (off and on), staff's own
  bookings and feed, own calendars, the bell and the Crew badge.
- **The freelancer's link:** the glance line, offers, today's card and
  running late, the call sheet, timesheets, days they can't work, documents
  and certificates, sharing their calendar, the feed, the office's details,
  the purchase list, stale links.
- **Kit going out and coming back:** Home's warehouse view, kit on a job and
  from the notes, shortages and subhire, vans, the pick list and picking on
  the phone, scanning out and back, faults on return, repairs, the show
  pack, trackers, all of it offline.
- **Looking after the stock:** the catalogue, a product and an item,
  places, labels and cable labels, counts and stocktakes, PAT testing,

- **People:** Crew › People, adding a person, a person's page and its tabs,
  photo, emails and Make main, combining, erasing, archiving, bringing the
  crew list in, departments, roles.
- **Home and getting around:** Home's four views for each role, Needs
  attention, the week, charts, Your tasks, Claude's to-do list, the Money
  card, the bar and sidebar, the Menu, area rows, breadcrumbs and the way
  up, On this screen, the theme, notifications and toasts, You, What's
  changed, the update bar, sign-in, a new phone's first copy, offline.
- **Admin and bringing things in:** Admin's tabs and every switch,
  Connections, Security, Status, History, Download everything, the
  calendar import screen, Tidy up, venues and the map, clients and their
  documents, the Current RMS bring-ins.
- **Across the app:** contrast, focus, headings, names for screen readers,
  tap targets, reduced motion, the colour grammar, states, wording, empty
  states for a first-day company, refusals, printing, phone fit, and page
  size and speed at full size.

Each flow as walked, with its verdict:

### Job flow

- **New job (enquiry) with client, venue and phases** (office, director): Quick, and it lands on the job page with Next saying Make a quote. Near-miss client and venue names make duplicates without a word.
- **Selling: Make a quote, then Record the client's answer, Confirm the job or Client said no (ADR 0103)** (office, director): The gate works and the yes lands in one tap. But Next points at a quote that the quote page itself says to leave until kit and crew are in, and confirming without a quote ticks 'Quote sent'.
- **Planning: crew, kit and vans from the Next dock** (office, head, director, staff crew): The words are right and the place stays put on phone and desk. Pressing it often looks dead, and staff are offered work they can't do.
- **Tasks: give one to someone, set a due day, late, Your tasks on Home** (office, head, staff crew, director): Giving and dating a task takes 3 taps and shows up straight away. Opening your own task from Home doesn't open it, and nothing on Home counts late or unowned tasks across jobs.
- **Going out, On the job, Back, deprep, closed (read from records)** (office, head, director): Deprep finally has a place. But when the kit wasn't scanned, the job's records contradict each other on one card, and nothing closes a job except a hand tick.
- **Cancel and reopen a job** (office, director): It works in 3 taps each way and nothing is lost. The same sentence appears twice, and nothing near it says how to reopen.
- **Jobs list: find one job, filter and sort, the fortnight fold, stage line** (office, director, head): Searching is instant and the stage line reads well. On a phone the controls push the list below the fold, and you can't filter by stage or by late.
- **Jobs > Board (kanban)** (office, director, head, staff crew): The card lines are good where they apply ('5 of 10 booked · 7 lines of kit, 1 short · 3 days left'). Jobs that never got closed off swamp both ends of the board, and on 'Next 8 weeks' too.
- **Planner week and month** (office, director, head): Clear, and the late-running and crew-short marks work. It shows no stage, so a job tomorrow with nothing picked looks like any other.
- **Home: Today and the week ahead with stage, Your tasks** (director, office, head, staff crew): The stage on Home is a real gain. Your tasks works for staff, but nothing counts late tasks for the office or a director.
- **Job from a past Current RMS quote (ADR 0102/0103)** (office): Inferred: the order is right (the existing job first). The one-Show-phase limit is an ADR decision.

### Quoting and money

- **Start a quote from #quotes (New quote)** (office, director): Quick and clear. The line under the form says how the job moves (enquiry, then quoted when sent).
- **Start or finish a quote from the job** (office, director): Works. The Next dock names the right action until the client answers (see the finding on the label after a yes).
- **Add lines with the product picker** (office, director): Finding by part of a name and by category works well, with clear labels like 'Audio · Speakers'. It is slow over many lines: three taps each, and the add row closes after every pick.
- **Rate rules Daily / 3 Day Week / Weekly / Once** (office, director): 3 Day Week reads well ('Charged 6 days'). Switching to Weekly keeps a day price as a week price without a word (finding 1).
- **Line and department discounts, Agree a total** (office, director): Clear and correct. Faults show when the field is left, and the words say what to do.
- **Packages** (office, director): Works. Availability is checked against what the package holds. The package's own price box names its unit, which the line's does not.
- **Crew travel and subsistence** (office, director): Rows like any other line, with the reason in the second line. Admin's travel card is clear. The pointers to Admin are plain text, not links.
- **Availability, subhire and margin on the draft** (office, director): Calm and informative. The margin shows only to the office and directors. Two different margins appear once the quote is sent (finding 2).
- **Autosave** (office, director): Nothing to press. A run of quick adds saves only after a 1.2 s pause, as designed.
- **Printed quote (print to PDF)** (office, director): Clean A4: header, client and contact, groups, 'less 15%', subtotal, agreed discount, VAT, total, all matching the screen. A 3 Day Week line prints 'Days 6' on a 3-day job with no explanation. An unpriced line prints as €0.00.
- **Sending by email (off state, then the switch on with no mailbox)** (office, director): The off state is honest. The switch can be turned on before the mailbox works, which turns every quote's main button into a dead end.
- **Mark as sent, the client's answer, confirm, invoiced, paid** (office, director): Every step is a record with Undo-like 'Take … back'. The job moves enquiry › quoted › confirmed. The job page's Next keeps the wrong label after the yes (finding 3).
- **Quotes page (#quotes)** (office, director): Finds anything quickly. The follow-up list includes quotes for jobs already over (finding 4).
- **Past Current RMS quotes on a quote** (office, director): Off state only: the empty states are honest. 'Put it on <job>'s quote' couldn't be walked without the data.
- **Ask Claude on a draft quote (off, not set up)** (office, director): Hidden where it can't work, with a plain reason in Admin. Quiet, as live will be.
- **Jobs › Money and Home's Money card** (director, office): Numbers agree: November €26,189 confirmed of €29,729 quoted, €175,325.70 accepted and unpaid (€77,259.00 invoiced + €98,066.70 not), Avoca's €8,610.00. Home's 'below zero' sentence isn't a link.
- **What each role sees of money** (head, staff crew, freelancer): Locked down. Heads see none of their department's money; that is on purpose (ADR 0062 rejected 'Heads see their department's quotes'), not a gap.

### Crew booking

- **Ask for crew on a job (phase, role, rate, department, reply-by, local crew count)** (office, head): Good. The defaults are sensible (reply-by and department), the confirmation line says where the new call landed, and a head is told the office sets the rate rather than shown an empty box.
- **Crew › Calls, narrowed by department, the windowed list** (office, head, director): The list reads well, and the 'On today' line keeps today's running-late calls apart. A head starts on their own department in Crew needed only; the answers above it are everyone's.
- **Offer a call to someone (messages on and off)** (office, head): Booking works fine with messages off: the offer is made and the link says so, in 3 taps and 140 ms. But the follow-up card always opens at the foot of the page, far from the call.
- **Settle answers to check (confirm, release, agree a counter, note a decline)** (office, head): Each row's wording is clear. But every Confirm or Release takes the office to the foot of the page, or opens the Tell card out of sight, and nothing sorts by date or narrows by kind.
- **A freelancer's counter-rate** (office, head): Clear and correct for both roles, as ADR 0046 says.
- **Call sheet: open and print to PDF** (office, head): Held up. The Print button gives a clean one-page A4 sheet: contact, where, running order, crew with numbers, vans, kit, client. Ctrl+P instead prints the whole screen with its chrome (3 pages).
- **Running late (ADR 0028)** (office, head): Quick and plain, with 44 px targets. Held up.
- **Clashes and checks in To sort out (ADR 0069)** (office, head): Reads clearly with a clash/check pill. Nothing in it covers a call still short close to its date.
- **The planner as a booking tool** (office): It works as a way into booking. The cell opens the top of the job rather than its crew, so it's one tap more than it needs to be.
- **Crew › Leave: ask, approve as a head, allowance, days in lieu** (staff crew, head, office): The rules and warnings are good: year end, who else is off, booked that day. The approver's queue sits a long way below their own leave, isn't narrowed to their department, and a request into next January can't be made until the office opens the year.
- **Crew › Timesheets with the switch on and off (ADR 0090)** (office, head, freelancer): Off holds everywhere. #crew/timesheets says 'Timesheets are off' and where they're turned on. On, the figures add up and read clearly, but the lists give dates with no year, going back nine months.
- **Staff crew's own bookings on Home › Me, and their calendar feed** (staff crew): Calm and short (1,157 px). The feed card can't be reached in passcode mode; from the code, it gives Subscribe and Copy with plain help.
- **Notifications bell and list (ADR 0058)** (office, head, staff crew): Bundling works. But someone else's yes that fills your call is listed 'About you' with no word on what it means for your offer.
- **Crew tab badge: what fills the number** (office, head, staff crew): Right for the office. For a head it is the whole company's number, and it includes their own leave request, which they can't decide.

### Freelancer's link

- **Open the link from a text (glance line, what's waiting)** (freelancer): Clear and quick: 9 KB on the wire, first paint in about 0.5 s on a 400 kbps, 400 ms link, with no horizontal scroll at 390 px in either theme. The glance's 'Next' says 'Thu 5 Nov' rather than 'Today', and it counts today's job as a timesheet to send before it's worked.
- **Today's card and running late** (freelancer, office): Works in 3 taps, and the message lands in the card. 'Running late?' is still offered after 'I'm here now', which is harmless.
- **Answer an offer: accept, some days, counter a rate, decline** (freelancer, office): The wording is right and a busy day is never ticked for them. But after each answer the card's message, and on a refusal the card's title, sits under the sticky glance bar. Unticking every day on an accepted booking says 'or press Decline', and that card has no Decline.
- **An offer whose call filled elsewhere** (freelancer): Fixed since 20 Oct 49: the job is named and the message is in view at the top.
- **Call sheet (who to ring, venue, times, print)** (freelancer): Plain and right on screen, and it shows no other crew's numbers to ordinary crew. Printed from a phone or laptop in dark mode, the text comes out light grey on white paper.
- **Timesheets: days, extras, send, approved figures; and with the switch off** (freelancer, office): Sending works in 2 taps plus typing. Every confirmed booking with no age limit asks for a timesheet (to-send rows back to Mon 6 Apr 2026). The approved page still lacks what an invoice needs and never says it was paid. With the switch off, the old address reads as if they'd lost the booking.
- **Days they can't work: add, overlap, bad dates, remove** (freelancer, office): The checks are fixed since 20 Oct 50. The thank-you is drawn at the top of a 10,900 px page while the browser lands at the section, and Remove throws them back to the top.
- **Documents and certificates: send a photo** (freelancer, office): Wording and types are good. A photo over about 10 MB breaks to a browser error rather than the app's own 'over 10 MB' message, and a new document's expiry accepts a day already gone.
- **Sharing their own calendar (ADR 0044)** (freelancer): Clear refusals beside the field, with what was pasted kept. The promise of what's kept is said before anything is pasted.
- **Calendar feed** (freelancer): Works. The page tells them it's 'fine in a calendar you share', but every event carries their day rate.
- **Purchase list on the link (ADR 0108)** (freelancer, office): Simple and solid in both themes. Names show as ADR 0108 decided, with no contact details, and the download carries what they asked for and voted for.
- **Their details (phone)** (freelancer): Fine.
- **Stale and wrong links** (freelancer): Archived and erased are handled. A trailing slash shows raw JSON, and a stray character from a message reads as if the link were dead.
- **Page at volume (20 offers, 40 bookings) and on a slow link** (freelancer): 17,878 px and 2,106 words on a phone (15,289 px at desk), down from 29,764 px on 20 Oct. Bookings fold to a line each after three (4,817 px for 40), but 20 offers are 11,435 px of full cards. 110 KB of HTML goes over the wire as 8.3 KB brotli; load is 0.7 s at 400 kbps.
- **What the office sees back** (office): What the freelancer does on the link reaches the office in words straight away.

### Kit out and back

- **Home, Warehouse view on a load-out morning** (warehouse, head): Clear and quiet. The next thing (the job going out tomorrow) is at the top, and each tile lands on the list that explains it. Nothing from the trackers shows here.
- **Pick list going out (typed numbers, the scanner's path)** (warehouse, head): Quick and forgiving. A double tap on Count out the 16 or on Undo recorded once. But the list is long, Undo pollutes the job's progress, and a damaged-but-fit item is sent out with no word about its fault.
- **Scanning with the camera (judged from web/src/stock/Scanner.tsx)** (warehouse, head): A label held still is guarded against double reads. Two labels inside the frame at once can flip-flop (A, B, A), so B's answer and its Undo are replaced by 'A is already out'. Stopping and restarting the camera resets the guard, which is harmless here.
- **Kit coming back, check-in and a fault on return** (warehouse, head): Works. Damage can be reported only on the item just scanned back: after tapping Back in the list, or once the next label is read, the button is gone.
- **Repairs list (#stock/repairs)** (warehouse, office, head): Clear grouping. Back from <job> and the note are on each row, and today's report showed at once (offline too). Within a group the newest report is at the bottom.
- **A job's kit and vans as office (desk) and warehouse (phone)** (office, warehouse): Readable. The same page also says 'Picked and loaded 84 of 122 out' in Where it is: two numbers for one thing, because of how Undo is counted.
- **Kit in the notes (#jobs/notes-kit)** (office): Clear. Not walked further than the list.
- **Show pack** (warehouse, office): Plain and reachable by the warehouse. The download wasn't opened.
- **Trackers: kit to check (judged from code; reports need TRACKERS_KEY)** (warehouse, office, head): Off by default. Once on, kit away from the warehouse is shown only to someone who opens Notifications on their own.
- **Offline: pick, scan out, report a fault, reload, back online** (warehouse, head): Nothing was lost, and the waiting count is honest once a change has been made. Before the first change, the top bar says Up to date while there is no signal.

### Stock

- **Stock area root (catalogue, pick lists, kit short)** (office, head, warehouse, staff crew): Clear and ordered as ADR 0017 asks, with going out first. On a phone the first product starts about 560 px down and Kit short is the last thing on a 3,000 px page.
- **Find an item by number** (office, head, warehouse, staff crew): Fast and forgiving: 2 taps and a few keys, with the result answering as you type.
- **Catalogue: filter, sort, Show all, windowed list, Back** (office, head, warehouse): Filter and sort read well, with counts in each choice. Back throws away Show all and lands on Kit short.
- **Product page (details, on jobs, items, cable labels, change details, rack-mount)** (office, head, warehouse): Tabs and counts are clear and On jobs says 'Enough, with 14 to spare'. 'In all 7 2 can't go out' misreads, and every product, speakers included, has a Cable labels tab.
- **Item page and fault fixing** (office, head, warehouse, staff crew): Everything about one item is on one page and staff get only the buttons they can use. Certificates' empty state says 'Nothing here yet. None yet: …'.
- **Repairs (sort out a fault)** (head, warehouse, office): Earlier finding 93 is fixed: can't-go-out comes first, with one quiet button each. The fix itself is armed although it can be reversed.
- **Places and a place's page** (office, head, warehouse): The list is fine. A busy bay's page is a seventeen-screen chip wall.
- **Labels: set numbers aside, print a run, spreadsheet for a label maker** (office, head, warehouse): Earlier finding 101's wording is fixed and the print sheet carries only the labels: 12 pages of 50 × 25 mm, each with a QR code, number and company name.
- **Cable labels with length and colour (ADR 0095, 0106)** (office, head, warehouse): The length reads well ('3 m ORANGE' in a black band) and the label prints alone. The code defaults to Barcode on products with no stock code, so the label prints with no code.
- **Counting: rolling count of a due place** (head, warehouse): Earlier finding 43 is partly fixed (counts per group, sticky Finish), but all 589 numbers are still drawn (15,186 px). A foreign item scanned gets a good amber answer.
- **Testing: a PAT batch** (head, warehouse): Scanning a batch is quick and forgiving. A pass records with no tester, and the page ends in a 1,800 px block of product names.
- **Racks and the rack builder (palette, press to place, 3D, side, rack sheet)** (head, office, warehouse): Building a rack works on a phone with one thumb. While kit is picked, neither the 'Holding' line nor the rack is on screen, and the 3D box takes the page's scroll.
- **Purchase list: ask and vote (ADR 0077, 0108)** (staff crew, head, office, freelancer): Asking takes three taps and confirms in place. The department list is the crew's (LX, Rigger, SFX), not the stock's, and the empty state doubles its words.
- **Bring in the stock list (ADR 0026, 0056)** (office, director, staff crew, head): For the office, the clearest bring-in in the app. Staff and heads who reach the address get the whole screen and are refused only after mapping every column.
- **What staff crew and freelancers can do in stock** (staff crew, freelancer): Staff can report and ask, but some pages still tell them to do what they can't. A freelancer who finds broken kit has nowhere to say so, though their role lists reporting a fault.

### People

- **Find a person in Crew › People (search, sort, filter)** (office, director, head, staff crew): Fast at a desk. On a phone it is a long scroll first. Search misses apostrophe variants, phone numbers and email addresses.
- **Add a person (first and last names)** (office, director, head): Short and clear. It gave no warning that a Ben Quinn was already there, so a duplicate was made at once.
- **A person's page and its tabs (Coming up, Details, Documents, Worked, Their link)** (office, director, head, staff crew): Much shorter than before, and the tabs fade at the edge. Archive sits in Coming up as a link, which is an odd home for it but works.
- **Photo on a person's page** (office): Works. The consent hint is good.
- **Make main email** (office): Swaps at once and is reversible the same way. Nothing on screen says it was done.
- **Combine people from the person page** (office): The question is clear about bookings and addresses. It doesn't say the other phone number goes, and the warning line isn't a link.
- **Combine people from Jobs › Tidy up** (office): Clear, but it defaulted to keeping the empty new record over the one with 53 bookings.
- **Archive a person** (office, director): Good: reversible, done at once, with the way back said. The refusal says 'release' while the button is called Withdraw.
- **Erase a person on request** (director): Two deliberate steps, plain words, says it can't be undone. The Goes list leaves out the photo, their own calendar and the other addresses.
- **Bring in the crew list (#crew/import)** (office): Problems block the bring-in until fixed or skipped, and duplicate rows are caught. A matched person's email is replaced silently, and the row shows kind, level and rate the server won't apply.
